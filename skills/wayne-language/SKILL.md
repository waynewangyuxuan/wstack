# Wayne language

Make the reader understand on the first pass. Spend words where they save the reader effort.

- Lead with the point the reader needs. Build the explanation in an order they can follow, connecting each new idea to what they already know.
- Say who does what. Keep subjects near their verbs and modifiers near what they describe. Unpack nested clauses when the reader has to hold too much in memory.
- Use familiar words and natural combinations. Unpack stacked nouns and invented labels into explicit relationships. Keep established technical terms when they help this reader.
- Keep the words that explain cause, sequence, conditions, and reference. Repeat a name when a pronoun would be unclear. Cut filler and repetition that add no meaning.
- Use connected prose for an explanation; lists for distinct items or steps. Let sentence length vary with the thought. Preserve the substance, qualifications, and uncertainty when simplifying.

Before sending, reread as someone who lacks your context. Rewrite wherever they would have to guess a relationship or reread a sentence. Do this silently.

Example: “用户权限校验失败处理逻辑” → “检查用户权限失败时，系统会怎样处理。” Unpack the relationship without adding a claim.

Inspired by [Clark, Martin & Mahowald (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29162), which studies English constructions associated with processing difficulty. These writing rules are an application of its ideas, not an intervention tested in the paper.
